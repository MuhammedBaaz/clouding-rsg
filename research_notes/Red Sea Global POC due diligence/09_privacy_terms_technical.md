# Privacy notices, terms, consent mechanisms and public technical footprint of Red Sea Global guest-facing sites and booking channels

Research date and access date for every source below: 12 October 2026 (viewer geolocation as reported by the consent tool: Egypt).

Method notes that apply to the whole file:

- Pages were read either as raw HTML fetched with curl ([FETCHED-HTML]) or rendered in a browser tab and inspected read-only ([OBSERVED-LIVE]). No forms were submitted, no accounts created, no chat started, no booking search run.
- Verbatim quotation is deliberately limited to short fragments (under 15 words each). Everything else is close paraphrase with the clause location and URL. Check exact wording at the URL before relying on it in a legal document.
- None of the RSG-family legal documents carries a visible "last updated" or effective date. Where a date is given below it comes from the document itself; "none shown" means the page displays no date and the fetched HTML contains no date metadata that I could find.
- redseaglobal.com and amaala.com returned an HTTP 403 Cloudflare challenge page (title "Just a moment...") to curl and WebFetch for most URLs. Those pages were read in a normal browser tab instead. No challenge was solved or bypassed.
- The browser profile was shared with other researchers. A OneTrust consent cookie with only the strictly-necessary group enabled was already stored for visitredsea.com and amaala.com when I arrived, so I did not see the first-visit banner render. Banner wording and categories were read from the OneTrust configuration the page itself had loaded.
- Labels: [OFFICIAL-DOC], [OBSERVED-LIVE], [FETCHED-HTML], [INDICATIVE], [THIRD-PARTY], [CONFLICT], "Not verified - requires ...".

## 1. What do the privacy notice, cookie policy, terms of use and booking terms on redseaglobal.com, visitredsea.com and amaala.com disclose?

### Takeaway
There are four distinct RSG-family document sets, not one. Three different legal entities appear depending on the document: Red Sea Global (corporate site and destination privacy notices), Regenerative Escapes Tourism Company (RETC, the contracting party for bookings made on visitredsea.com) and Red Sea Global Hospitality (RSGH, controller for Turtle Bay Hotel). None of the documents shows a last-updated date, none names a DPO, and none mentions chatbots or an AI assistant; the destination privacy notice does disclose AI-based profiling and a right concerning solely automated decisions.

### Inferences
- A guest who books a stay on visitredsea.com appears to be dealing with three named parties across the documents: RETC as contracting party and merchant, "Red Sea Global and its subsidiaries" as data controller, and the hotel as RETC's supplier. The documents do not explain how those roles relate for personal data.
- The shared CR number 1010916245 in the visitredsea.com footer and in the booking terms suggests the footer CR is RETC's, not RSG's. Not confirmed by any document that states it.
- The amaala.com legal pages look like copies of the visitredsea.com pages (same wording, visitredsea.com named as the website, "Visit Red Sea" in an Arabic page title).

### Gaps
- Last-updated or effective dates for every RSG-family document: not shown on any page. Not verified - requires RSG to supply version history or dated PDFs.
- Clause-by-clause comparison of Arabic and English versions was not done; only existence, length and spot checks. Not verified - requires a bilingual legal read.
- Whether a DPO has been appointed and how to contact them: not stated in any document.
- Whether hotel operators receive guest personal data, and on what basis: no document names operators as recipients.
- redseaglobal.com has no separate cookie policy page that I could find; the privacy policy says more information is available "here" but the link target was not captured.
- The Privacy Center web form fields and the rights it supports were not inspected (form not opened).
- Legal identity and address of RETC beyond name, company type and CR number: not given in the booking terms.
